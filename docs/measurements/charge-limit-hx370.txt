Charge limit efficacy, FRANMGCP09 (Ryzen AI 9 HX 370), BIOS 04.02, EC lilac-4.0.2
2026-09-18

Limit set to 80% through fw-helperctl (EC command 0x3E03). The battery charged
on AC from below 80%; the user watched it reach 80% and stop. Read afterwards:

  ACAD online=1                      (on AC throughout)
  BAT1 status=Not charging
  BAT1 capacity=80
  BAT1 charge_now=3593000 / charge_full=4490000   = 80.02%
  fw-helperctl status: battery 80% (Not charging), charge limit 80%

Sampled every 10 s for 90 s after that:

    0s  Not charging  cap=80  charge_now=3593000  current_now=364000  voltage=16827000
   10s  Not charging  cap=80  charge_now=3593000  current_now=180000  voltage=16822000
   20s  Not charging  cap=80  charge_now=3593000  current_now=88000   voltage=16811000
   30s  Not charging  cap=80  charge_now=3593000  current_now=40000   voltage=16803000
   40s  Not charging  cap=80  charge_now=3593000  current_now=7000    voltage=16800000
   50s  Not charging  cap=80  charge_now=3593000  current_now=6000    voltage=16797000
   60s  Not charging  cap=80  charge_now=3593000  current_now=6000    voltage=16796000
   70s  Not charging  cap=80  charge_now=3593000  current_now=5000    voltage=16794000
   80s  Not charging  cap=80  charge_now=3593000  current_now=4000    voltage=16792000
   90s  Not charging  cap=80  charge_now=3593000  current_now=5000    voltage=16786000

Verdict: charging stops at the limit. charge_now does not move, and the
voltage relaxes, as a cell does once current stops.

Difference from the Intel board: there current_now read 0 at the stop. Here
the first read, seconds after the stop, was 553 mA, and it decayed to ~5 mA
over ~40 s. On this board current_now lags behind status, so a single read
of current_now right after the stop can look like charging continues.
Judge by charge_now or by status, not current_now.

not the scripted test. Not yet tested on FRANMGCP05.
